Rex — travel recommendations with the friend attached

Friend-attributed travel recommendations with no feed, no likes, no rankings. A product I design, build, and run.

The short version

The best travel advice is already in your friends’ heads, scattered across old texts and screenshots. Rex collects it, keeps who told you attached to every place, and surfaces the overlap: the spot three friends recommended independently. No feed, no likes, no rankings.

Rex is live at rexthis.com. I design it and build it, and two friends have shaped the product along the way with sharp input.

What it does

Collect recommendations

  • Save a place with a status (want to go, liked, top pick), a type, notes, and tags.
  • Attribution. Every pin comes from someone and links to their account once they join. A friend’s note stays in their words.
  • Paste a texted list. Rex finds each place and queues it for review, credited to the friend who sent it.
  • Import saved places from Google Maps, by Takeout file or shared list link.

Plan a trip

  • The map is the app. Filter by person, type, status, or tag, from a country down to a neighborhood.
  • Overlap. When several friends recommend a place, its pin shows the count. No ranking.
  • Friend layers. See every friend’s places in a city at once, minus the ones you’ve saved.
  • Ask a friend for their picks in a city, starting from what they’ve already mapped.

Track where you’ve been

  • Check in with one tap. “Been” is its own fact, and you can rate on the spot.
  • Passport. Each city you visit earns a custom stamp, in a flip-through book at a public URL.
  • Log book. A private, dated record of every visit. It opens only from the passport and place pages, so it never becomes a feed.

Share what you know

  • Share a filtered view. “My Lisbon recs” as a link anyone can open and save from in one tap.
  • Invite links bring a friend straight into your circle.

Product decisions

City first, not list first. The first version opened onto your own list and felt like every other places app. A July working session with a friend reframed it: people open Rex to plan a trip, so the front door is a city and the friends who know it. Your own list becomes the passport you build and show off. That reframe reorganized the roadmap.

No ranking. Restaurant apps make you rank places against each other. A Brooklyn pizza place and a café in Amman aren’t comparable, and forcing the comparison makes people stop logging. Rex’s bar is simpler: a friend was confident enough to recommend it. Overlap is a number, never a sort order.

“Been” is a fact, not an opinion. Status is an opinion ladder. Having been is a separate axis, derived from check-ins, so a place can be been and never rated. The product goes looking for that state.

The passport fills itself in. A check-in writes the city stamp, so nobody types one in. It never overrides what a person told Rex directly.

The database is the security boundary. Friend visibility, share links, and private contact data are enforced with Postgres row-level security, not client code. An app bug can’t leak a friend’s private notes because the database won’t return them.
