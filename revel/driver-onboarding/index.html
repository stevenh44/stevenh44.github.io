A driver onboarding funnel, bootstrapped then rebuilt

Launched v1 on Webflow and Zapier to hit the contractor launch date, then rebuilt it on a "restrictions" model: 2× activations, 2.5× qualified supply, ops out of nearly every step.

The short version

When Revel moved its drivers to an independent-contractor model, every new driver had to clear a dozen hurdles before a first ride: license checks, insurance, a background check, tax forms, a payout wallet, and signed agreements. I built every version of that funnel: first on no-code tools to hit the launch date, then piece by piece inside our own platform. The rebuild doubled new driver activations, grew qualified driver supply 2.5×, and took ops out of nearly every step.

The problem

Employee hiring ran through a slow, expensive chain of HR software: Greenhouse, an onboarding tool, Dayforce for payroll, then our backend. None of it fit contractors, who don’t go through payroll.

By mid-2024 the contractor process had become three separate task lists with dependencies between them, tracked in a spreadsheet and managed by hand. Drivers couldn’t see where they stood. Applicants lived in a recruiting tool until “hired,” so we couldn’t message them, see where they dropped off, or attribute a finished driver to the ad that found them.

Launch on no-code tools

The contractor launch had a fixed date, so I built the first funnel myself instead of queuing it for engineering, from Webflow, Zapier, and our existing Greenhouse account:

  • A Webflow form captured the application and marketing attribution.
  • A Zap checked the TLC license against NYC open data, age and experience minimums, and insurer eligibility, and auto-rejected anyone who couldn’t legally drive for us before human review.
  • Marking a driver “hired” in Greenhouse fired a webhook that created them in our platform with their remaining tasks.

It onboarded contractors from day one with no added ops work, and showed which parts of the funnel deserved real engineering.

Rebuild on restrictions

The core idea: replace the checklist with zero or more machine-readable reasons a driver can’t drive yet.

Each restriction has a type (it blocks either taking passengers or renting a vehicle) and an owning system. A driver with any open restriction is automatically “pending.” When the last one clears, they’re eligible without anyone touching their record. I scoped the first release as the foundation plus one integration, and kept sensitive identity data with specialist vendors rather than in our database.

We then attached each hurdle as its own project:

HurdleHow it clears
TLC + DMV license validityChecked against NYC open data automatically
Insurance approvalRead from the insurer by TLC number
Background checkDriver invited to Checkr; result arrives by webhook
Tax identity (W-9)Hosted form with Abound
Payout walletBranch wallet created at sign-up
Services + arbitration agreementsDocuSign envelopes, cleared on completion
Photo, address, phone, emailCaptured and verified in-app

Drivers photographed their driver’s and TLC licenses in the app. An OCR pipeline on AWS extracted the numbers for the same open-data and insurer checks. The photos went to an ops review queue, the one deliberately human step: a machine can read a license number, but a person should look at the license.

Every restriction change was an event, so marketing automation could reach drivers mid-funnel: you’re two steps from driving — finish your background check. Drivers saw their own list in the app and stopped calling ops to ask what was left.

Restrictions outlived onboarding. The same primitive later took a driver offline when their TLC license was revoked or their driving was flagged as unsafe. No new system, just a new reason.

Retire the scaffolding

With restrictions carrying the logic, Greenhouse was down to three jobs: holding attribution data, serving as a waiting room, and firing one webhook. Its contract was ending. We replaced it with an applicants table in our backend, a web sign-up that creates the driver directly, deduplication by phone and email, and attribution through Segment. Zapier and Greenhouse both went away.

The spec’s success criteria: we no longer use Greenhouse; we maintain at least comparable funnel tracking. Both held, and tracking was the bigger win. Before, application and driver records were joined by email, phone, or name, and funnel numbers were only as good as the joins. After, the application, candidate, and driver each had an ID pointing to the others, and funnel measurement became a query. The goal was never the license cost. It was owning the funnel end to end, so we could measure it, message into it, and change it without a vendor in the way.

Results

  • 2× new driver activations in the first ~4 months
  • 2.5× qualified driver supply
  • Apply to first ride cut from at least 2.5 weeks to about a week, and a few days for the fastest drivers
  • Manual ops work removed from nearly every onboarding step
  • Drivers reachable by automated messaging from application, not from hire
