Performance pay for W2 drivers

Tied the employee-driver shift premium to performance, and showed drivers their numbers a week before they touched pay. Revenue-generating time rose 5%, trip compliance improved, and the limit of hourly pay came into view.

The short version

Revel’s New York rideshare drivers were employees paid by the hour. In 2023 I led the change that tied the shift premium to performance, and drivers saw their numbers a week before those numbers touched pay. Driver behavior shifted fast: revenue-generating time rose 5% and trip compliance improved. It also showed where W2 pay ran out.

The starting point

Pay was an hourly base, plus shift premiums for high-demand windows, plus tips. A great driver and a poor one earned the same premium for the same hour. Revel also needed positive gross margin, and premium pay was the lever within reach.

A premium tied to performance

In fall 2023 we made the premium conditional on a short list of pass/fail metrics a driver could control: availability during paid time, missed rides, pickup delay, attendance, and low ratings (excluding those the passenger attributed to the car or the app).

The rollout mattered more than the formula. Showing the scoreboard before it counted made this a pay change, not a pay dispute.

The PM on my team made pay legible before a shift with a pay simulator, a pay calculator for open shifts, and persistent projected pay.

Standing as a system record

Next we gave the numbers a memory. A driver’s standing came from strikes per shift over the last six shifts: under 0.5 was good, 0.5 to 1 a warning, 1 or more an employment review. Standing didn’t affect pay. The app showed it on every shift, so when ops had to act, the record already existed for Legal and HR. Exempting a stat after the fact recalculated every later shift.

Results

  • +5% revenue-generating time
  • Better trip compliance

What I’d do differently

Explain the why earlier and more often. The week of visible stats helped, but each metric’s rationale deserved more than a message and a reminder.

Launch with looser thresholds. Ours were too strict and had to be loosened against real shifts. I’d start looser and tighten.

Where W2 ran out

Performance management was the biggest lever W2 offered. It helped, but it didn’t fix the incentive: an hourly driver earned the same for zero completed rides as for fifteen, and drivers still found ways to avoid rides. Revel carried all the utilization risk, and no hourly pay design could shift it.
